I've been thinking too much again. It's time to just wrap up my last trip. As I mentioned, there was also my teacher's workshop in Helsinki. When I started driving to classes in Helsinki every week over twenty years ago, the classes were big, with four groups at different stages of the slow set, and the rooms were full. At that time, almost everyone was new to me, but it was also easy to blend in. There was something similar this time, with familiar faces and new students.

In one class, we paid extra attention to how to begin. Even before moving, feel your body from feet to your face. Relax. Be sensitive. Listen your body. Settle into your body. Know your posture. Stand straight. Feel ready. Follow. Finish each movement. Slowly. Stop. Continue. Do faster. Do differently. Break your patterns.

This time, I picked up on *study your movements*, as my teacher said, and made it to my theme. Or simply: study your moves and moving. Where are you at the moment? How did you get there? Where are you going next? Know when and where to stretch or relax. There's a fine line between holding on or letting go. Know the difference.

One very common practice is to simply repeat a single movement many times, on both sides. For example, with *single whip*, 单鞭 *dān biān*, you can practice repeatedly by pushing, 按 *àn*, again from the end posture and turning back, one side after another. Or with *retreat and seal*, 如封似闭 *rú fēng sì bì*, you can first repeat the movement several times on one side, turn around by circling your hands, and repeat on the other side.

Good students study carefully. Every teacher and master has also been a student first. I've been scratching out a book about Tai Chi, and my working title for it has been *A Student*. It must say something about how important I consider studying to be. The master's family also calls their school an academy, which emphasizes the study of the art. Be diligent in your studies.

Think your body as an instrument, and play it at your best. Study your body. Study your mind. Study your behavior. Study the nature - like a snake and a bird - the natural ways. Don't compare yourself to others. Compare how you've been doing before. Challenge yourself. Do more than usual. Give yourself a chance. Who could you become if you stopped trying so hard to be who you think you are now?
